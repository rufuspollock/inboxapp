# Why not just use X (other tool)

Here is an example real-life question from a user

> Agree a simple capture facility is useful but unclear why you wouldn't create it using standard Obsidian functionality (Bases, Virtual Content plugin, etc) which I just did in around 15 minutes. For low friction, assumes you keep Obsidian always running.
>
> Useful for thought capture would be a way to send Markdown from a web page to Obsidian, creating a new note (a sort of 'reverse FlowerShow').

My response was:

Great question — I actually use Obsidian all the time.

One important bit of context is that I often have several Obsidian vaults open at the same time. So even before getting to the inbox itself, there’s friction in just finding the right vault — out of three or four — that actually contains my inbox.

Then, once I’m in my main vault, where the inbox lives, I found that whenever I switched there I’d hit a kind of friction period. I’d open the inbox and then get distracted by things I hadn’t processed yet — sometimes from earlier that day, sometimes from the previous day. That was the pattern.

I know there are quick-capture plugins for Obsidian, and I’ve tried to configure them. But in practice I was still switching to Obsidian, tabbing between vaults, finding the right one, then switching to the relevant note, and only then capturing the thought. Even if each step is small, together it was enough friction to break the flow.

What I wanted was something really minimal, clean, and automatic: always a blank sheet of paper, no visible backlog, and absolutely minimal friction. That’s what I wasn’t getting with Obsidian.

What I actually do now is copy everything out of Inbox App at the end of the day (there’s a one-click copy for that) and paste it into Obsidian, where I then process it properly. So Obsidian is still my main system of record — Inbox App just gives me a deliberately “dumb”, interruption-free capture layer in front of it.
